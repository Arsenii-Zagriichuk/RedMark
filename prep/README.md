# prep/

Owner: Person 2 (data and drone matcher).

Crops, reprojection, tiles and layer files for layers 1 to 3; the Jezero pack. Lists its data files in `data-manifest.yaml`.

## Jezero pack (P2-04)

`REDMARK_DATA=~/redmark-data uv run python -m prep.jezero.build_pack` builds the pack Person 4 keys the drone answer key on. Flights and margins are in `jezero/pack.yaml`. It writes `$REDMARK_DATA/jezero/pack/` (crops, frame images, overlays, `frames.csv`), `sites/jezero.yaml` and the Jezero entries in `data-manifest.yaml`.

Every Jezero position is in the site frame `+proj=eqc +lat_ts=18.4663 +lon_0=0 +R=3396190`: the published map's sphere and origin with true scale at Jezero, so map metres are ground metres. It is the frame of NASA's own Ingenuity waypoints, so a position here equals NASA's published Easting and Northing.

## Gale walk-area crop pack (P2-01)

`REDMARK_DATA=~/redmark-data uv run python -m prep.gale.build_pack` cuts every Gale source to the walk area and writes `$REDMARK_DATA/gale/crops/` (the crops, `rover-positions.csv`, `chemistry-apxs.csv`, overlays and `pack.json`), `sites/gale.yaml`, the Gale entries in `data-manifest.yaml` and copies of the overlays in `gale/evidence/`. It downloads the CRISM cube, the APXS tables and PLACES' every-drive positions once. Once the crops exist it reads only them, so a rerun takes under a minute and does not need the 50 GB photo map; the first run takes a few minutes more to checksum it.

- **Checked before it is published.** The build writes the candidate pack to `gale/crops.next/` and replaces `gale/crops/` only if every check passes; `sites/gale.yaml`, the manifest and the evidence copies are written after that. A failed check leaves what teammates read as it was.
- **Walk area.** Set in `gale/walk_area.yaml`, with the reasons for the choice: the habitat sits on gentle open ground west of Curiosity's boxwork drive, the walk area is the 4 × 4 km square holding the 2 km walk radius, and the crops add 500 m round it. The build measures the ground round the habitat and base camp (mean slope, share of cells steeper than 10°, distance from Curiosity's track) under `walk_area_figures` in `pack.json`. A new walk area is a rerun, not a code change.
- **Sources.** The photo map, terrain, THEMIS and PLACES files are expected at the top of `REDMARK_DATA` under their published names, as Person 3's horizon grid also reads them; the build downloads the rest into `gale/source/` and `places/`. Every source, CRISM included, is in the site projection, so each crop is a window on its source's own pixel grid, never resampled; CRISM keeps all 60 bands with their names. The window is snapped outward to whole pixels, so the 1 m terrain crop starts on its source's half-metre grid.
- **Frame check.** The photo map was made from images taken before Curiosity landed, so it shows no rover tracks to check by eye. The build runs three checks instead and fails if any does. First, the orthophoto and terrain grids that PLACES localised Curiosity on must sit a whole number of pixels from our maps' grids, within 1 cm. Second, the terrain's height at each of Curiosity's stops in the crops must match PLACES' elevation there: the median within 0.5 m, and 9 in 10 stops within 0.5 m. On the boxwork slopes, a sideways shift of the terrain would fail this. Third, the photo map's brightness must line up with the terrain's shading within 5 m: both come from the same HiRISE stereo images, and this pins the photo map itself, which a whole-pixel shift would carry past the first two checks (moved 50 m east, it reads 52.5 m off and fails). Results are in `pack.json` under `frame_check`.
- **THEMIS offset, recorded, not corrected.** The build correlates THEMIS thermal inertia with the photo map's brightness and the terrain's slope for every 25 m shift up to 300 m. It records, under `thermal_inertia_offset` in `pack.json`, the best move by each measure and its correlation, with and without the move. Both measures point north-west, a few THEMIS pixels, but they differ by about 100 m east–west and the correlations are weak (|r| at most 0.34), so the crop is left as published. P2-03 should read the walkability layer with that uncertainty in mind.
- **Crop extents.** Each crop covers the asked bounds on its own source grid, so the edges differ by up to one source pixel: the terrain crop sits half a metre off the whole metre, and THEMIS reaches 50 m further north and south. Read each crop's own transform.
- **CRISM: one cube, moved onto the base map.** One CRISM targeted cube covers the whole crop: FRT0000B6F1 (9 July 2008, 18 m), found with the PDS ODE footprint search; two others cover about half. The crop holds its 60 refined summary parameters, the per-pixel mineral indices; P2-03 picks the highlights. CRISM's pointing is not tied to HiRISE. The build correlates CRISM's 770 nm reflectance with the photo map's brightness for every 6 m move up to 300 m. Moving CRISM 156.6 m west and 64.0 m north raises r from 0.31 to 0.78, so the crop is cut with that move. It is applied only when r reaches 0.6; THEMIS's best is 0.34. The cube's edge is ragged: in every band, 540 of the crop's 18 m cells (0.7%), all in the western margin, hold no data. Inside the walk radius 59 of the 60 bands are complete; BD3200 has its own gaps, 88 cells inside the walk radius. `crism_coverage` in `pack.json` counts the gaps band by band, for the crop, the walk area and the walk radius. The move and both r values are under `crism_offset` in `pack.json`, and the overlay shows the habitat before and after. CRISM's own files disagree by one pixel (18 m): its PDS3 label, read by GDAL's PDS driver, puts the cube one pixel further west and north than its ENVI header does. The build reads the ENVI header, which matches the label's own corner longitude and latitude, and the measured move corrects either reading.
- **Chemistry: APXS where the rover stood.** `chemistry-apxs.csv` holds every APXS measurement made inside the crops, with concentrations as published: oxides in wt%, Ni, Zn and Br in ppm, each with its error. The build finds from PLACES the sols Curiosity spent inside the crops (3843 onwards), reads every APXS concentration table overlapping them, and keeps each measurement by where the rover stood. APXS tables give no position: each measurement sits where the rover's last drive before its sol ended, from PLACES' `localized_interp.csv`. APXS is on the arm, so the target itself is within about 2 m. Each row also lists its source files, whose names carry the rover's site and drive. Where those files name the row's own sol, they give the same pose as placing by sol, every time. In the sol 3193 to 4133 table they are scrambled: target, sol and start time agree with each other, while the file names point hundreds of sols away. So the file names are not used to place. Both counts, and the tables read, are under `chemistry` in `pack.json`. ChemCam targets are not included.

## Layer 1 at Gale: base map tiles and slope (P2-02)

`REDMARK_DATA=~/redmark-data uv run python -m prep.gale.build_layers` reads the P2-01 crops named in `sites/gale.yaml` and writes, under `$REDMARK_DATA/gale/`, `rasters/slope.tif` (for the route engine), `layers/base/` and `layers/slope/` (for the map), each with a `provenance.json` (script, commit, and the checksums of its input files and of `sites/gale.yaml` as the build read it, since the config sets the bounds and names the inputs), and two overlays in `layers/overlays/`, copied to `gale/evidence/`. It names the slope raster as `files.slope` in `sites/gale.yaml`. It takes about 15 seconds.

- **Tiles.** Both layers cover the walk area (`bounds` in the site config), laid out by the layer contract: at `max_zoom` one pixel is `resolution_m`, each lower zoom halves it, and tile (x, y) counts from the north-west corner. Tiles are 250 px, which divides the 4 km walk area exactly at 25 cm (64 tiles across, zoom 6) and at 1 m (16, zoom 4), so no tile reaches past the edge. Where one would, its outside part is transparent, and tiles wholly outside are not written.
- **Base map.** The photo map crop at its own 25 cm, unresampled (the walk area sits a whole number of pixels into the crop), as grey JPEG tiles at quality 90: 5,461 tiles, about 100 MB.
- **Slope.** Degrees from the 1 m terrain by central differences on the terrain's own grid, which sits half a metre off the whole metre. Map metres are used as ground metres; at Gale they differ by under 0.3% east–west (team plan). `rasters/slope.tif` covers the whole crop on that grid, so the route engine reads it unresampled. The slope layer resamples it bilinearly onto the walk area's whole-metre grid and colours it in bands with edges at 5, 10, 15, 20 and 30°, so the route's two slope limits (15° and 20°) are band edges. Each zoom down takes the mean of 2 × 2 slopes before colouring, so every zoom shows only legend colours; averaging the colours would make blends the legend doesn't hold. A cell with a missing height, or next to one, is unknown and transparent; a pixel drawing on an unknown cell is unknown too, so a hole is never filled with a guess.
- **Overlays.** `slope.png` shows the slope tiles on the photo map, over the walk area and at full detail round the habitat. `base-tiles.png` pastes the base tiles together using only `layer.json`, beside the crop placed by its own GeoTIFF transform, with Curiosity's PLACES stops on both. `app-base-map.png` and `app-slope.png` are Person 1's map on these layers: the habitat marker sits at the photo's centre, and the 2 km walk-limit circle touches its edges. Take app screenshots with a fixed viewport (puppeteer `setViewport`), not Chrome's `--screenshot` flag, which leaves room for window chrome and stretches the map canvas against the markers.
- **Rolling out.** `sites/gale.yaml` names `files.slope`, so the route engine needs `gale/rasters/slope.tif` in every data folder it reads. A folder with only the P2-01 crops makes `POST /route` fail with HTTP 500. Copy `gale/rasters/slope.tif` and `gale/layers/` into each `REDMARK_DATA` (shared folder, USB copy) before that checkout pulls this config, or run the build there.
- **Stub.** `contracts/stubs/stub-data/gale/rasters/slope.tif` is a 656-byte stand-in (40 × 40 cells of 100 m, 1° everywhere over the walk area), so the route engine finds the file `sites/gale.yaml` names when `REDMARK_DATA` is unset. It is force-added past the `*.tif` ignore rule. It is 1°, not 0°, because on exactly flat float32 ground the route's planning-speed check reads 2.00000005 km/h against its 2 km/h limit.
